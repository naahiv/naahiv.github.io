The question which Dold-Kan answers is quite natural from the perspective of algebraic topology. Namely, if $A$ is a simplicial abelian group, we can try to define a chain complex by setting its group in degree $n$ to be $A_n$ and taking the boundary map to be the alternating sum of the face operators $d_i$. This defines a functor

$$
\mathrm{Moore}\colon\sab\longrightarrow\ch(\ZZ)
$$

from simplicial abelian groups to non-negatively graded chain complexes of abelian groups. Given the resemblance of the objects in either category, one might ask whether it is an equivalence.

The answer is no because of the degeneracy operators $s_i\colon A_{n-1}\to A_n$, whose images make the Moore complex too large. Instead, we will take its quotient by the subcomplex generated by degeneracies. The Dold-Kan correspondence says that the resulting construction is an equivalence of categories. The proof will consist of constructing an inverse functor.

We begin by constructing this quotient and finding an isomorphic description which will help prove the theorem. Then we define the inverse in two steps: first make a semisimplicial abelian group, and then freely add degeneracies. Throughout, we work with abelian groups.

## The normalized Moore complex {#normalized-moore-complex}

:::definition
A *non-negatively graded chain complex* over $\ZZ$ is a sequence $A=A_\bullet$ of abelian groups

$$
\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{n+1}} A_n
\xrightarrow{\partial_n}A_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots
\longrightarrow A_1\xrightarrow{\partial_1}A_0
$$

such that $\partial_n\partial_{n+1}=0$ for every $n\geq1$. A morphism of such complexes $f\colon(A,\partial)\to(B,\partial)$ is a sequence of homomorphisms $f_n\colon A_n\to B_n$ making

$$
\begin{CD}
A_n @>{\partial_n}>> A_{n-1} \\
@V{f_n}VV @VV{f_{n-1}}V \\
B_n @>{\partial_n}>> B_{n-1}
\end{CD}
$$

commute for every $n\geq1$. We write $\ch(\ZZ)$ for the corresponding category.
:::

On the other hand, let $\sab=[\simp^\op,\ab]$ be the category of simplicial abelian groups. Here $\simp$ is the category of finite nonempty ordered sets $[n]=\{0,\ldots,n\}$ and order-preserving maps. We write $d^i\colon[n-1]\mono[n]$ for the injection omitting $i$, and $s^i\colon[n]\epi[n-1]$ for the surjection identifying $i$ and $i+1$. Applying a simplicial object gives the face and degeneracy maps $d_i$ and $s_i$ in the opposite direction.

The goal is to construct an equivalence of categories $\sab\simeq\ch(\ZZ)$. We will first set up three intimately related functors $\sab\to\ch(\ZZ)$ and learn their properties.

### 1. The Moore complex {#moore-complex}

Let $A$ be a simplicial abelian group. We associate to it a chain complex $(A_*,\partial)$ with the same abelian groups and with

$$
\partial_n=\sum_{i=0}^n(-1)^i d_i.
$$

:::proposition
The assignment $A\mapsto A_*$ defines a functor $\sab\to\ch(\ZZ)$.
:::

:::proof
We first need $\partial_{n-1}\partial_n=0$. From the definition,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{n-1}\partial_n
&=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i+j}d_jd_i \\
&=\sum_{i\leq j}(-1)^{i+j}d_jd_i
  +\sum_{i>j}(-1)^{i+j}d_{i-1}d_j \\
&=\sum_{i\leq j}(-1)^{i+j}d_jd_i
  -\sum_{l\leq k}(-1)^{k+l}d_kd_l=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the second step we used the simplicial identities, and in the third we set $k=i-1$ and $l=j$.

For functoriality, note that a simplicial map $f\colon A\to B$ is a natural transformation, so

$$
\begin{CD}
A_n @>{d_i}>> A_{n-1} \\
@V{f_n}VV @VV{f_{n-1}}V \\
B_n @>{d_i}>> B_{n-1}
\end{CD}
$$

commutes. Since $\partial_n$ is a linear combination of face maps, the same square obtained by replacing $d_i$ with $\partial_n$ still commutes.
:::

:::definition
The chain complex $A_*$ is the *Moore complex* associated to the simplicial abelian group $A$.
:::

:::example
Let $X$ be a topological space and $\mathrm{Sing}(X)$ its singular simplicial set. Taking the free abelian group on the singular simplices in each degree gives a simplicial abelian group $\ZZ[\mathrm{Sing}(X)]$. Its Moore complex is the usual singular chain complex $C_*(X;\ZZ)$, whose homology is that of $X$.
:::

### 2. The reduced Moore complex {#reduced-moore-complex}

Recall that an $n$-simplex $x\in A_n$ is *degenerate* if there is $y\in A_{n-1}$ such that $s_i(y)=x$ for some $0\leq i\leq n-1$. Set

$$
DA_n=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\im(s_i\colon A_{n-1}\to A_n),
\qquad DA_0=0.
$$

We claim these groups form a subcomplex $DA_*\subset A_*$. We need to show that $\partial(s_i x)\in DA_{n-1}$. Since $d_i s_i=d_{i+1}s_i=\id$, the two corresponding terms cancel, and the simplicial identities give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial(s_i x)
&=\sum_{j\ne i,i+1}(-1)^j d_j s_i(x) \\
&=\sum_{j>i+1}(-1)^j s_i d_{j-1}(x)
 +\sum_{j<i}(-1)^j s_{i-1}d_j(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus we have represented $\partial(s_i x)$ as a sum of degenerate simplices. Empty sums are understood to be zero.

:::definition
The *reduced complex* associated to a simplicial abelian group is the quotient complex

$$
(A/DA)_*=A_*/DA_*.
$$

We write $q\colon A_*\epi(A/DA)_*$ for the canonical quotient map.
:::

This is functorial because simplicial maps preserve degenerate simplices, giving our second functor $\sab\to\ch(\ZZ)$. The quotient map $q$ is then a natural transformation.

### 3. The normalized complex {#normalized-complex}

Let $A$ be a simplicial abelian group, and set

$$
NA_n=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\ker(d_i\colon A_n\to A_{n-1}),
\qquad NA_0=A_0.
$$

We take the boundary map $NA_n\to NA_{n-1}$ to be $d_0$. This is well-defined because if $x\in NA_n$ and $0<i\leq n-1$, then

$$
d_i d_0(x)=d_0d_{i+1}(x)=0.
$$

Moreover, $d_0d_0(x)=d_0d_1(x)=0$ implies that $(NA_*,d_0)$ is a chain complex. It is a subcomplex of the Moore complex: the inclusion $i\colon NA_*\to A_*$ is a chain map, since

$$
\partial_n(x)=d_0(x)+\sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^i d_i(x)=d_0(x)
\qquad(x\in NA_n).
$$

:::proposition
The assignment $A\mapsto NA_*$ is functorial, and the inclusion $i$ is a natural transformation.
:::

:::proof
If $f\colon A\to B$ is simplicial and $x\in NA_n$, then for $i\geq1$,

$$
d_i(f_n(x))=f_{n-1}(d_i(x))=0.
$$

Thus $f_n$ carries $NA_n$ to $NB_n$. Taking $i=0$ in the same naturality identity shows that these restrictions define a chain map. Naturality of the inclusion follows directly.
:::

:::definition
The *normalized complex* is the functor

$$
N_*\colon\sab\longrightarrow\ch(\ZZ),\qquad A\longmapsto NA_*.
$$
:::

The Dold-Kan theorem is that $N_*$ is an equivalence. The first step in the proof will be that the reduced complex is isomorphic to the normalized complex.

### Comparing the two normalizations

We will need a lemma first. For $0\leq k\leq n$, define

$$
N_kA_n=\bigcap_{i=k+1}^n\ker d_i,
\qquad
D_kA_n=\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\im s_i.
$$

In particular, $N_0A_n=NA_n$ and $D_0A_n=DA_n$, whereas $N_nA_n=A_n$ and $D_nA_n=0$. The indexing is chosen so that we impose the equations $d_n=0,d_{n-1}=0,\ldots,d_1=0$ in that order.

:::lemma[Two exact sequences]{#lem:exact-sequences}
Let $A$ be a simplicial abelian group, $n\geq1$, and $0\leq k<n$.

1. The sequence

   $$
   0\longrightarrow A_{n-1}/D_kA_{n-1}
   \xrightarrow{s_k}A_n/D_{k+1}A_n
   \longrightarrow A_n/D_kA_n\longrightarrow0
   $$

   is well-defined and exact.

2. The sequence

   $$
   0\longrightarrow N_kA_{n-1}
   \xrightarrow{s_k}N_{k+1}A_n
   \xrightarrow{\phi}N_kA_n\longrightarrow0
   $$

   is well-defined and exact, where $\phi(x)=x-s_kd_{k+1}x$.
:::

:::proof
For (1), the simplicial identities give, for $i\geq k$,

$$
s_k s_i=s_{i+1}s_k.
$$

Hence $s_k(D_kA_{n-1})\subset D_{k+1}A_n$, so the first map is well-defined. It is injective: if $s_kx\in D_{k+1}A_n$, apply $d_k$. Since $d_k s_k=\id$ and, for $i>k$,

$$
d_k s_i=s_{i-1}d_k,
$$

we obtain $x\in D_kA_{n-1}$. The final map is a quotient map, so it is surjective, and its kernel is the image of $s_k$ because

$$
D_kA_n=\im s_k+D_{k+1}A_n.
$$

For (2), if $y\in N_kA_{n-1}$ and $i>k+1$, then

$$
d_i s_k y=s_k d_{i-1}y=0.
$$

Thus $s_k$ maps into $N_{k+1}A_n$, and it is injective because $d_{k+1}s_k=\id$. If $x\in N_{k+1}A_n$ and $i>k$, then

$$
d_i d_{k+1}x=d_{k+1}d_{i+1}x=0,
$$

so $d_{k+1}x\in N_kA_{n-1}$. Subtracting $s_kd_{k+1}x$ therefore preserves the equations defining $N_{k+1}A_n$ and also kills $d_{k+1}x$. This proves that $\phi$ takes values in $N_kA_n$.

Now $\phi s_k=0$, while $\phi(x)=x$ for $x\in N_kA_n$, so $\phi$ is onto. Finally, if $\phi(x)=0$, then $x=s_kd_{k+1}x$, with $d_{k+1}x\in N_kA_{n-1}$ as just proved. This establishes exactness in the middle.
:::

:::theorem[Normalized and degenerate simplices]{#thm:normalization-splitting}
For a simplicial abelian group $A$, the map

$$
NA_*\oplus DA_*\longrightarrow A_*
$$

induced by the two inclusions is a natural isomorphism of chain complexes. In particular, the composite

$$
NA_*\xrightarrow{i}A_*\xrightarrow{q}(A/DA)_*
$$

is an isomorphism.
:::

:::proof
It suffices to prove the second statement. Indeed, once $qi$ is an isomorphism, the map $(qi)^{-1}q\colon A_*\to NA_*$ is a retraction of $i$, and its kernel is $DA_*$.

We prove that for every $n\geq0$ and $0\leq k\leq n$, the composite

$$
N_kA_n\longrightarrow A_n\longrightarrow A_n/D_kA_n
$$

is an isomorphism. We do this by induction on $n$, and within a fixed degree by descending induction on $k$.

For $n=0$ there is nothing to prove. In any degree the case $k=n$ is the identity of $A_n$. Suppose we know the assertion in degree $n-1$ and for $k+1$ in degree $n$. Put the two exact sequences of :ref[lem:exact-sequences] into the diagram

$$
\begin{CD}
0 @>>> N_kA_{n-1} @>{s_k}>> N_{k+1}A_n @>{\phi}>> N_kA_n @>>> 0 \\
@. @VVV @VVV @VVV @. \\
0 @>>> A_{n-1}/D_kA_{n-1} @>{s_k}>> A_n/D_{k+1}A_n @>>> A_n/D_kA_n @>>> 0.
\end{CD}
$$

The vertical arrows are induced by inclusion followed by quotient. The left square commutes by the definition of how $s_k$ passes to the quotients. The right square commutes because

$$
\phi(x)=x-s_kd_{k+1}x\equiv x\pmod{D_kA_n}.
$$

The first nonzero vertical arrow is an isomorphism by induction on $n$, and the second by induction on $k$. Taking cokernels shows that the third is an isomorphism as well. At $k=0$ this proves the desired comparison $NA_n\cong A_n/DA_n$.

All the maps used are natural in $A$, and the comparison is already a chain map. Thus the resulting isomorphism is an isomorphism of chain complexes, natural in $A$.
:::

The decomposition in :ref[thm:normalization-splitting] will be important in the proof of Dold-Kan. Before using it, however, we need to construct an inverse to $N_*$.

## The Eilenberg-MacLane functor {#eilenberg-maclane-functor}

To prove Dold-Kan, we will explicitly describe a functor

$$
K\colon\ch(\ZZ)\longrightarrow\sab
$$

inverse to the normalized complex construction. Its construction factors through the category of *semisimplicial* abelian groups.

:::definition
Write $\simp_{\mathrm{inj}}\subset\simp$ for the wide subcategory consisting of injective order-preserving maps. The category of *semisimplicial abelian groups* is

$$
\ssab=[\simp_{\mathrm{inj}}^\op,\ab].
$$

Thus a semisimplicial abelian group has face maps satisfying the face identities, but no degeneracy maps are specified.
:::

Let $(A,\partial)$ be a chain complex. There is an associated semisimplicial abelian group $K^s(A)$ defined as follows:

1. $K^s(A)_n=A_n$.
2. An identity injection induces the identity homomorphism.
3. For a nonidentity injection $j\colon[m]\mono[n]$, the map $K^s(A)(j)\colon A_n\to A_m$ is zero unless $m=n-1$ and $j=d^0$, in which case it is $\partial_n$.

To see that $K^s(A)$ is a presheaf, consider a composite

$$
[l]\xrightarrow{j}[m]\xrightarrow{i}[n].
$$

We must check $K^s(A)(ij)=K^s(A)(j)\circ K^s(A)(i)$. If one of the injections is an identity, this is immediate. Otherwise the composite has codimension at least two, so its associated map is zero. The composite of the two associated maps is also zero, except possibly when both injections are $d^0$; in that case it is $\partial_{n-1}\partial_n=0$.

This construction is functorial in the chain complex: a chain map commutes with the nonzero face map because it commutes with $\partial$, and commutes with all the other face maps because those are zero.

:::definition
The functor

$$
K^s\colon\ch(\ZZ)\longrightarrow\ssab
$$

just constructed is the *semisimplicial Eilenberg-MacLane functor*.
:::

It remains to provide the second half of the composite $\ch(\ZZ)\to\ssab\to\sab$. Recall the following elementary result about $\simp$.

:::lemma[Epimonic factorization]{#lem:epimonic}
Every map $u\colon[m]\to[n]$ in $\simp$ has a unique factorization

$$
[m]\xrightarrow{\pi}[k]\xrightarrow{i}[n]
$$

in which $\pi$ is surjective and $i$ is injective. Moreover, $\pi$ is a split epimorphism.
:::

:::proof
Factor $u$ through its image, which is uniquely order-isomorphic to $[k]$ for some $k$. The image has cardinality $k+1$, so no other $[k]$ can work; order preservation then determines both maps. An order-preserving section of $\pi$ is obtained by choosing the least element in each fiber.
:::

:::proposition[Adding degeneracies]{#prop:adding-degeneracies}
There is a canonical functor $E\colon\ssab\to\sab$ defined in degree $n$ by

$$
E(F)_n=\bigoplus_{\phi\colon[n]\epi[k]}F_k.
$$

The sum is taken over all surjections in $\simp$ with source $[n]$.
:::

:::proof
Let $u\colon[n]\to[n']$ be a map in $\simp$. To define $u^*\colon E(F)_{n'}\to E(F)_n$, we define it on the summand associated to each surjection $\phi'\colon[n']\epi[k']$. Factor $\phi'u$ into a surjection followed by an injection, obtaining the unique commutative square

$$
\begin{CD}
[n] @>{u}>> [n'] \\
@V{\phi}VV @VV{\phi'}V \\
[k] @>{v}>> [k'].
\end{CD}
$$

Here $\phi$ and $\phi'$ are surjective, and $v$ is injective. On the $\phi'$-summand, define $u^*$ to be

$$
F_{k'}\xrightarrow{F(v)}F_k
$$

with image in the $\phi$-summand of $E(F)_n$; all its other components are zero.

It remains to check composition. For maps $[m]\xrightarrow{u}[n]\xrightarrow{w}[p]$ and a surjection $\theta\colon[p]\epi[l]$, perform the factorization twice:

$$
\begin{CD}
[m] @>{u}>> [n] @>{w}>> [p] \\
@V{\psi}VV @V{\phi}VV @VV{\theta}V \\
[j] @>{a}>> [k] @>{b}>> [l].
\end{CD}
$$

The vertical maps are surjections and the bottom maps are injections. By uniqueness, the outer rectangle is the factorization of $\theta wu$. Hence the two successive maps on the $\theta$-summand are $F(a)F(b)=F(ba)$, exactly the map prescribed for $(wu)^*$. Identities are respected for the same reason.

Finally, for a semisimplicial map $\eta\colon F\to G$, define

$$
E(\eta)_n=\bigoplus_{\phi\colon[n]\epi[k]}\eta_k.
$$

Naturality of $\eta$ with respect to the injection $v$ in the first square shows that this commutes with every $u^*$. Thus $E(\eta)$ is a simplicial map, and the construction respects identities and composition.
:::

:::definition
The composite

$$
K\colon\ch(\ZZ)\xrightarrow{K^s}\ssab\xrightarrow{E}\sab
$$

is the *Eilenberg-MacLane functor*. In particular,

$$
K(A)_n=\bigoplus_{\phi\colon[n]\epi[k]}A_k.
$$
:::

The Dold-Kan correspondence says that $K$ is both a left and a right inverse to $N_*$, up to natural isomorphism. We can now prove one direction.

:::theorem[Normalizing the Eilenberg-MacLane construction]{#thm:half-dold-kan}
For a chain complex $A$, inclusion of the identity summand gives a natural isomorphism

$$
A\xrightarrow{\sim}N_*K(A).
$$
:::

:::proof
The only surjection $[n]\to[n]$ is the identity, so the definition of $K(A)_n$ contains $A_n$ as a distinguished direct summand. First we show that this summand lies in $N(KA)_n$.

For the identity summand, the factorization used to define the face $d_i$ is simply

$$
\begin{CD}
[n-1] @>{d^i}>> [n] \\
@V{\id}VV @VV{\id}V \\
[n-1] @>{d^i}>> [n].
\end{CD}
$$

Thus $d_i$ on $A_n$ is $K^s(A)(d^i)$, with image in the identity summand of degree $n-1$. It is zero for $i\geq1$ and is $\partial_n$ for $i=0$.

Next we identify the degenerate part of $K(A)_n$. A degeneracy $s_i$ takes the summand indexed by $\psi\colon[n-1]\epi[k]$ identically to the summand indexed by $\psi s^i\colon[n]\epi[k]$. Therefore its image lies in the sum of the nonidentity summands. Conversely, every nonidentity surjection $\phi\colon[n]\epi[k]$ identifies some adjacent pair $i,i+1$, and hence factors as $\phi=\psi s^i$. Its entire summand is in the image of $s_i$. Consequently,

$$
D(KA)_n=\bigoplus_{\substack{\phi\colon[n]\epi[k]\\k<n}}A_k,
\qquad
K(A)_n=A_n\oplus D(KA)_n.
$$

By :ref[thm:normalization-splitting], we also have

$$
K(A)_n=N(KA)_n\oplus D(KA)_n.
$$

Since $A_n\subset N(KA)_n$, these two decompositions imply $N(KA)_n=A_n$ as subgroups of $K(A)_n$. Explicitly, if $x=a+d$ belongs to $N(KA)_n$, then $d=x-a$ lies in $N(KA)_n\cap D(KA)_n=0$.

We already checked that $d_0$ on this summand is $\partial_n$, so these identifications respect the differentials. A chain map acts on each identity summand by its degree-$n$ component, which proves naturality.
:::

## Finishing Dold-Kan {#finishing-dold-kan}

We now complete the proof of the classical correspondence.

:::theorem[Dold-Kan correspondence]{#thm:dold-kan}
The functor

$$
N_*\colon\sab\longrightarrow\ch(\ZZ)
$$

is an equivalence of categories, with inverse given by $K$.
:::

We have already seen one direction in :ref[thm:half-dold-kan]. To finish, we construct a natural isomorphism

$$
\mu\colon K\circ N_*\longrightarrow\mathrm{Id}_{\sab}.
$$

For a simplicial abelian group $A$, define

$$
\mu(A)_n\colon K(NA_*)_n
=\bigoplus_{\phi\colon[n]\epi[k]}NA_k\longrightarrow A_n
$$

on the $\phi$-summand by the composite

$$
NA_k\mono A_k\xrightarrow{\phi^*}A_n.
$$

:::proposition{#prop:comparison-natural}
The maps $\mu(A)_n$ define a natural transformation $KN_*\to\mathrm{Id}_{\sab}$.
:::

:::proof
First consider an injection $v\colon[k]\mono[k']$. On $NA_{k'}$, the induced map $v^*$ agrees with the map prescribed by $K^s(NA_*)$:

- If $v$ is the identity, both maps are the identity.
- If $v=d^0$, both maps are the differential $d_0$.
- Otherwise $v$ omits some $j\geq1$, so it factors through $d^j\colon[k'-1]\mono[k']$. Its induced map therefore factors through $d_j$, which vanishes on $NA_{k'}$.

For the last point, an injection omitting at least two elements necessarily omits a positive element, so this also covers all injections of codimension at least two.

Now let $u\colon[n]\to[n']$, and factor $\phi'u=v\phi$ as in :ref[prop:adding-degeneracies]. On the $\phi'$-summand, applying the simplicial map of $KN_*A$ and then $\mu(A)_n$ gives

$$
NA_{k'}\xrightarrow{v^*}NA_k
\mono A_k\xrightarrow{\phi^*}A_n.
$$

By contravariance this is $(v\phi)^*=(\phi'u)^*=u^*(\phi')^*$, which is exactly what we obtain by applying $\mu(A)_{n'}$ and then $u^*$. Thus $\mu(A)$ is simplicial.

If $f\colon A\to B$ is a simplicial map, then $f_n\phi^*=\phi^*f_k$, and $f_k$ carries $NA_k$ to $NB_k$. This is precisely naturality of $\mu$ on each summand.
:::

Proving :ref[thm:dold-kan] now consists of showing that $\mu(A)_n$ is bijective in every degree. Surjectivity is easier. By :ref[thm:normalization-splitting], we have $A_n=NA_n\oplus DA_n$. The identity summand of $K(NA_*)_n$ maps onto $NA_n$. Inductively, every element of $A_{n-1}$ is in the image of $\mu(A)_{n-1}$, and compatibility with degeneracies then puts every $s_i(A_{n-1})$ in the image of $\mu(A)_n$. These subgroups generate $DA_n$, proving surjectivity. The induction starts in degree zero, where $\mu(A)_0$ is the identity.

For injectivity, the same exact sequences used in normalization allow us to keep track of all possible relations among the degenerate summands.

:::lemma[Normalization detects injectivity]{#lem:normalization-injectivity}
Let $f\colon B\to A$ be a simplicial homomorphism. If $Nf\colon NB_*\to NA_*$ is injective in every degree, then $f$ is injective in every degree.
:::

:::proof
We induct on the degree $n$. In degree zero the assertion is the assumption, since $NB_0=B_0$. Suppose $f_{n-1}$ is injective. For each $0\leq k<n$, :ref[lem:exact-sequences] gives a commutative diagram with exact rows:

$$
\begin{CD}
0 @>>> N_kB_{n-1} @>{s_k}>> N_{k+1}B_n @>{\phi}>> N_kB_n @>>> 0 \\
@. @V{f_{n-1}}VV @V{f_n}VV @VV{f_n}V @. \\
0 @>>> N_kA_{n-1} @>{s_k}>> N_{k+1}A_n @>{\phi}>> N_kA_n @>>> 0.
\end{CD}
$$

The left vertical map is injective by induction on $n$. The right vertical map is injective when $k=0$ by the hypothesis on $Nf$.

If the right vertical map is injective, so is the middle one: if $x\in N_{k+1}B_n$ maps to zero, its image $\phi(x)\in N_kB_n$ maps to zero, and hence $\phi(x)=0$. Exactness gives $x=s_k y$ with $y\in N_kB_{n-1}$. Then $s_k f_{n-1}(y)=f_n(x)=0$. Since $s_k$ and $f_{n-1}$ are injective, $y=0$, and hence $x=0$.

Starting with $k=0$ and increasing $k$, we conclude that $f_n$ is injective on every $N_kB_n$, up to $N_nB_n=B_n$. This completes the induction.
:::

:::proof[Proof of the Dold-Kan correspondence]
Apply :ref[thm:half-dold-kan] to the chain complex $NA_*$. It identifies

$$
N_*K(NA_*)\cong NA_*
$$

by the identity summands. On these summands, $\mu(A)$ is simply the inclusion $NA_n\mono A_n$. Therefore, after normalization, $N\mu(A)$ is the identity of $NA_*$ under the displayed identification. In particular, it is injective in every degree.

By :ref[lem:normalization-injectivity], $\mu(A)$ is levelwise injective. We have already shown that it is levelwise surjective. Thus it is an isomorphism of simplicial abelian groups. The construction is natural in $A$, so

$$
KN_*\cong\mathrm{Id}_{\sab}.
$$

Together with $N_*K\cong\mathrm{Id}_{\ch(\ZZ)}$ from :ref[thm:half-dold-kan], this proves the theorem.
:::

The correspondence gives, in particular, the canonical decomposition

$$
\bigoplus_{\phi\colon[n]\epi[k]}NA_k\xrightarrow{\sim}A_n,
\qquad
(x_\phi)_\phi\longmapsto\sum_\phi\phi^*(x_\phi).
$$

Thus the normalized complex retains precisely the information needed to recover the entire simplicial abelian group, including its degeneracies.

For more details and the extension beyond abelian groups, see the [paper version, *Extending the Dold-Kan correspondence*](/extending-dold-kan.pdf).
